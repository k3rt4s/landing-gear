# Keywords and applicant tracking systems

This chapter covers what these systems really do, how to read a posting for the
language it is searched against, and how to align your materials without lying.

## What the system actually is

### No system scores your resume and rejects it before a person looks

An applicant tracking system is the software an employer uses to receive applications,
store them, search them, and move candidates through stages. It is the employer's
system of record for hiring. When your application shows as rejected, a person set
that status, or the employer configured a specific rule that you triggered yourself,
most often by answering a question on the form. There is no universal hidden score
that a robot computes from your resume and uses to reject you before anyone sees it.
That belief is common and it is not accurate.

### Recruiters and coordinators are the users, you are not

The people who log into this system every day are recruiters, hiring coordinators,
and sometimes hiring managers. They search it, sort it, and read what is in it. You
never see the interface and never will. Keeping that in mind changes what you are
actually optimizing for, a human being's search and a human being's read, not a
machine's judgment.

### It is a search and ranking tool for the employer's own hiring record

Think of it the way you would think of a spreadsheet or a database with a search box.
The employer put your application into that database. A recruiter can search it by
title, skill, or years of experience, and can sort the results. That is ranking
within a list the employer already controls. It is not a machine that disqualifies an
applicant on its own before a person opens the record.

## What real automation does exist

### Knockout questions reject an answer you gave, not your resume

Many application forms include questions with a genuine automatic consequence, for
example work authorization, a required licence or clearance, a location or shift
requirement, or a stated minimum number of years. If your answer does not meet the
condition the employer set, the system can flag or reject the application on that
basis. That is real automation, and it is worth naming plainly. It is also something
you can usually see, because you were the one who answered the question.

### Filtering and sorting ranks candidates, and vendors differ

A recruiter can pull up every application for a role and sort it by a search term, so
people who used that term appear near the top of what gets read first. That is a
ranking of an existing list, not a rejection of anyone below the top of it, and a
recruiter can still scroll or filter differently. Vendors differ enormously in what
they offer a given employer, so treat any specific claim about vendor behavior as
something to check against this employer's own application flow, not as a rule that
applies everywhere.

## What to stop doing

### Stop hiding or stuffing keywords anywhere on the page

White text on a white background, a hidden keyword block, a job description pasted in
a tiny font, and repeating terms you do not otherwise support all rely on the idea
that a robot scans invisible or padded text and a person never will. The text a
system extracts is the text a person eventually reads, and hidden or repeated text
found by a recruiter reads as an attempt at deception, worse than doing nothing.

### Stop paying for a third party resume score

A percentage score from a resume-scanning tool you found online has no relationship
to what a specific employer's system does with your application, because it is not
that employer's system and was never built from it. Paying for that number buys you a
number, not a better outcome with the employer you are applying to.

## What formatting is for

### Formatting exists so a person can search and read your record, not to pass a robot

These systems extract your resume into a structured record with fields such as
employer, title, dates, and skills. A resume that parses badly produces a garbled
record. The cost of that is real, but it is not rejection by a machine. The cost is
that a recruiter searching their own database on a title or a skill may not find you,
and a person who opens your record afterward sees a mess instead of your work. Clean
formatting is worth doing because of that, not because you are trying to slip past
software.

### Use one column, conventional headings, consistent dates, and selectable text

A single column reads correctly in far more contexts than a two or three column
layout, and conventional section headings such as `Experience` and `Education` are
easier for both a parser and a person to find. Use one consistent date format across
every entry rather than mixing styles. Your resume should be real, selectable text,
not a picture of text saved as an image, and keep anything that matters out of
headers, footers, text boxes, and tables, since content in those containers is the
most likely to be dropped or scrambled during extraction.

### Read the parsed preview when the application offers one, and fix it

Many application flows show you a preview of what they extracted from your resume
before you submit it. That preview is the record a recruiter will later search and
read, so treat it as the single best check available. If a field is missing, blank,
or wrong there, correct it before you submit.

## How to read a posting for its language

### Separate the requirements block from the mission and culture language

Most postings mix two kinds of writing, the requirements block, listing what the role
needs, and marketing prose about culture, mission, or growth. The requirements block
is usually written or approved by the person who will search for candidates later, so
it carries the language most likely to matter. Read it on its own, apart from the
surrounding prose.

### Pull the nouns, named tools, and standards out of the requirements block

Go through the requirements block and pull out the concrete nouns, the named tools
and platforms, the standards or certifications, and the conventional title used for
the role. These are the terms a recruiter is likely to type into a search box later,
because they are the terms the employer already used to describe the job.

### Notice which terms repeat, and which of your true terms are missing

When a posting uses the same term more than once, that repetition is a reasonable,
ordinary signal the term matters to whoever wrote it, not proof of anything
mechanical. Compare the posting's list against your resume and note which terms
describing work you have genuinely done are simply not written down anywhere. That
gap, not a missing score, is the actual problem worth fixing.

## How to align without lying

### Use the employer's word for something you have genuinely done

When a posting names something you have actually done and you have been describing it
with your own internal shorthand or a different synonym, switch to the employer's
word. The recruiter will search their own word, not yours, and using it costs you
nothing because the underlying work is real.

### Never add a term for something you have not done

When a posting names something you have not done, do not add that term to your
resume. A keyword you cannot support is the same kind of failure as an invented number
on a resume. You carry it forward into an interview with someone who actually does
that work and will ask you about it, and there is no good answer waiting for you
there.

### Put terms inside real lines, not a list appended at the bottom

Place the terms you can honestly claim inside your summary, inside a competencies
block such as `Skills`, and inside the achievement lines where they describe what you
actually did, rather than tacking a bare list of words onto the end of the page. A
term embedded in a true sentence reads as fact. A term sitting in an appended list
reads as an attempt to be found by something other than a person.

### Keep job titles conventional and save the creative title for the body

If your actual title was unusual or invented internally, list a conventional,
searchable title such as software engineer or operations manager as the entry
heading, and put the original internal title inside the entry body if you want it
recorded at all. A recruiter searching by a common title is more likely to find the
conventional version.

## Applying, and if you hear nothing

### Apply through the employer's own system when one is offered

When an employer has its own application portal, apply through it rather than through
a third party aggregator when you have the choice, since that is where the record the
recruiter actually works from is created and lives.

### One employer sees every application you send as one candidate record

Applying to many roles at the same employer typically produces one candidate record
that carries all of those applications, visible together to whoever reviews it. That
is a reason to be selective about which roles you apply to at a single employer,
rather than sending an application to every opening they have.

### If you are not hearing back, weigh competition, a gap, timing, or a role that never opened

When you hear nothing back, the honest possibilities are that there was real
competition for the role, that there is a genuine gap between your background and the
requirements, that timing worked against you, or that the role was never truly open
in the first place. Blaming an invisible algorithm is the comfortable explanation, not
the likely one, and it is worth resisting because it points you away from the changes
that would actually help.

## How much time this takes tonight

### If you have the whole day, do the parse check once, on the master resume

If you have unlimited time right now, read the parsed preview once against your
master resume, fix what is wrong, and treat that as done. You do not need to repeat
this check for every application afterward, since the underlying document is the same
one each time.

### If you have forty minutes, spend one evening on postings, one on the competencies block

If your time is limited to something like forty minutes a night, spend one evening
reading three postings closely for their language, and a separate evening updating
your competencies block and achievement lines to match what you found. You do not
need to re-check parsing every night, once the master document is clean it stays
clean.
